Ex. 2 – Sampling Mean and Variance

1. Setup

Each penetration attempt receives an integer score in {0,…,10} drawn from a Binomial(10, 0.5) distribution (P(X=j) = C(10,j)/210). Its theoretical moments are:

μ = 10·0.5 = 5,   σ² = 10·0.5·0.5 = 2.5

There are m hackers; each attacks n systems and obtains a trajectory of n scores. For every hacker the sampling mean and the sampling (unbiased, divisor n−1) variance are computed with Welford’s recurrences (Ex. 1); the biased version SS/n is also reported for comparison. Then the m values are averaged and compared with μ and σ². The experiment is repeated for several n.

3. Conclusions

Comparison with μ and σ². The average of the sampling means is very close to μ = 5, and the average of the sampling variances (divisor n−1) is very close to σ² = 2.5, for every n, even n = 2. Hence both are unbiased estimators: E[x̄] = μ and E[s²] = σ². The biased estimator SS/n systematically underestimates σ²: its average is ≈ (n−1)/n · σ², an error that is large for small n and vanishes as n grows. The divisor n−1 (Bessel’s correction) is needed because deviations are measured from x̄, which is itself fitted to the data (one degree of freedom is lost).

Why this is useful in inferential statistics. In practice μ and σ² are unknown and only one sample is available. Unbiasedness guarantees that, on average over repeated samples, x̄ and s² hit the true parameters, so they can be used as point estimates, and as building blocks of confidence intervals (x̄ ± t·s/√n) and hypothesis tests (t-test, χ²-test, ANOVA). Welford’s recurrences additionally provide them online and in a numerically stable way.

Effect of n. Although the averages stay on target for every n, the variability of the estimates across hackers shrinks as n increases: Var(x̄) = σ²/n, so the standard deviation of the sampling means decreases like 1/√n (the table’s “SD of means” matches σ/√n). Likewise Var(s²) decreases roughly like 1/n (for large n, ≈ (μ₄ − σ⁴)/n, where μ₄ is the 4th central moment). Larger n therefore makes each single hacker’s estimates more reliable and concentrated around μ and σ² (consistency, law of large numbers); the sampling distribution of x̄ also becomes closer to normal (central limit theorem).
